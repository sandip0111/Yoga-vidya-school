Why Mysore?

Understanding the Importance of This Sacred City

Every place has its own history, but some places carry a special relationship with a particular tradition.

Mysore is one such place for yoga.

During the reign of Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV, the city became an important centre for the study and development of yoga. The royal family supported traditional education, Sanskrit scholarship, physical culture, and yoga practice.

It was in this environment that one of the most influential yoga teachers of modern times, Sri T. Krishnamacharya, taught and developed his approach to yoga.

His teachings influenced several extraordinary teachers, including Sri K. Pattabhi Jois, B.K.S. Iyengar, T.K.V. Desikachar, and Indra Devi. Their contributions helped introduce different approaches to yoga to students throughout the world.

Today, Mysore continues to attract practitioners from different countries who come to study, practise, and deepen their understanding of yoga.

Studying yoga in Mysore allows you to connect with an important chapter of yoga’s living history.

The Yoga Lineage of Mysore

A Legacy That Influenced Modern Yoga

To understand the importance of Mysore, we must understand its relationship with the great yoga teacher Sri T. Krishnamacharya (1888–1989).

During the 1930s, Krishnamacharya taught yoga under the patronage of the Maharaja of Mysore. His teaching combined traditional yogic knowledge, Sanskrit learning, philosophical principles, breath regulation, and systematic physical practice. His work became one of the most influential foundations of modern postural yoga.

Among his students were several teachers who went on to establish influential yoga traditions:

Sri K. Pattabhi Jois

Known for developing and popularising the Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga system. His teachings established Mysore as an internationally recognised destination for dedicated Ashtanga practitioners.

B.K.S. Iyengar

One of the most influential yoga teachers of the twentieth century, known for his systematic approach to alignment, precision, and the therapeutic application of āsana.

T.K.V. Desikachar

Known for developing an individualised approach to yoga, emphasising the relationship between breath, movement, and the needs of each practitioner.

Indra Devi

An influential teacher who helped introduce yoga to international audiences, particularly in Europe and the Americas.

Each of these teachers contributed in a different way, but their connection with Krishnamacharya demonstrates the remarkable influence of the Mysore yoga tradition.

When you study yoga in Mysore, you are studying in a city closely connected with this important lineage.
